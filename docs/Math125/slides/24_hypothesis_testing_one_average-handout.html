24. Hypothesis Testing – One Mean

Hypothesis testing with one mean

1 One-sample test for mean, overview

The parameter of interest is \(\mu\), a population mean.

The statistic is \(\bar{x}\), a sample mean.

The alternative hypothesis is \(\mu > k\) or \(\mu < k\) or \(\mu \neq k\).

The null hypothesis is \(\mu=k\)

The standard error is \(SE = \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\)

The test statistic is \(t = \frac{\bar{x}-k}{SE}\)

2 The T-test, an added wrinkle

  1. The standard error in this case “should be” \(SE = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\), but we almost never know \(\sigma\). The sample stdev \(s\) is what we know, and it’s close.
  2. The \(Z\)-score \(Z = \frac{\bar{x}-k}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}}\) would be a normally distributed estimation error but our substitute \(t = \frac{\bar{x}-k}{s / \sqrt{n}}\) is not, because the denominator is subtly data-dependent.
  3. We shouldn’t compare a t-score to the normal distribution. We must use a “modified normal” distribution called a t-distribution, which accounts for the problem \(s \neq \sigma\).
  4. The “fix” depends on sample size. The \(t\)-distribution with \(n-1\) “degrees of freedom” is the right one.

3 Identifying a t-test.

A restaurant supplier predicts delivery of salmon fillets with an average mass of 600 grams. This week’s average is only 580 with a standard deviation of 30 (n=100). This is smaller than the agreed average size. Is this statistically significantly smaller?

  1. A mean (not a proportion) is compared.
  2. There is a hypothesis to check comparing the mean to baseline or another mean.
  3. We have a standard deviation from the dataset but no population standard deviation.

4 Example 1 – a fishy discrepancy

A restaurant supplier predicts delivery of salmon fillets with an average mass of 600 grams. This week’s average is only 580 with a standard deviation of 30 (n=100). This is smaller than the agreed average size. Is this statistically significantly smaller?

# Square root function needs import
from math import sqrt 

#Record givens
k = 600  # Null hypothesis theory
x = 580  # Observed mean
n = 100  # sample size
s = 30   # SAMPLE standard deviation.

SE = s / sqrt(n) 
t = (x - k)/SE # (observed - predicted) / SE

# Import stats for "t distribution" tool, not normal!
from scipy import stats

# Find p-value as (left) tail area on t distribution
pvalue = stats.t.cdf(t, 99) 
# Note t was negative so I didn't negate it.

print(t)
print(pvalue)
